Listen to the knee while it works.

KneeSound is a knee brace that listens to the joint and tracks how it moves during a five-minute sit-to-stand test. We are building it to help clinicians in Singapore catch knee osteoarthritis earlier and track it over time, with no radiation.

Why

An X-ray shows bone standing still.

Knee osteoarthritis is usually confirmed on an X-ray, an image of bone at a single moment. By the time the joint space has visibly narrowed, cartilage damage is well established. Physiotherapy, load management and weight change work best before that point, and today there is no quick, objective way to check a knee earlier.

KneeSound is designed to work alongside imaging. Its accuracy against an accepted reference standard has not yet been tested; that is the next stage of our work.

How it works

One brace, a few minutes, a fuller picture of the knee.

The brace records the knee through a standard movement test and turns what it picks up into a single score a clinician can read and compare over time.

Joint sound

Worn cartilage changes the sound a knee makes as it bends. The brace listens for it.

Load response

How the joint behaves as body weight goes through it during each stand.

Movement

How a person rises, sits and shifts weight to protect a sore knee.

These signals are combined into one knee health score, tracked against the person's own baseline.

The test

Five sit-to-stands from an ordinary chair.

A trained operator runs the session. The person being tested only needs to stand up and sit down at their own pace.

  1. Fit the brace

    The operator straps the brace over the knee. It takes about a minute.

    ~1 min

  2. Answer a few questions

    Height, weight, and a short questionnaire about knee pain and past injury.

    ~2 min

  3. Stand up five times

    The brace records through each rise and descent.

    ~1 min

  4. Read the result

    A knee health score, plus the change since the person's own baseline on repeat visits.

    ~1–2 min
5–7 minutesper person, start to finish. No imaging, no referral.

Who it's for

Built for the places older knees already visit.

Screening

Adults aged 60 and over

A quick check for people at risk of knee osteoarthritis, run where they already go. A score above threshold prompts referral to a clinician.

  • Active ageing centres
  • Polyclinics and GP clinics
  • Community pharmacies

Monitoring

Physiotherapists

An objective number to track a patient from visit to visit. Today that judgement rests on goniometry, palpation and what the patient reports.

  • Baseline at the first session
  • Trend through a treatment plan
  • Evidence to show patients their progress

KneeSound is intended as a screening and monitoring aid. It supports triage and does not diagnose or treat on its own.

Progress

Where we are, and what comes next.

We would rather show you the gaps than hide them. Here is what is done and what still has to be proven.

  1. Done

    Wearable knee brace, v0.2

    Redesigned from a camera-based station into a wearable knee brace.

    July 2026

  2. Done

    Early bench recordings

    First recordings on three people. Enough to shape the hardware, nowhere near enough to claim accuracy.

  3. Done

    Design checked against the literature

    Brace design reviewed against published studies before detailed CAD work.

    October 2026

  4. Next

    User testing with 30–40 older adults

    Includes people with existing knee OA or knee pain. Excludes anyone with a knee replacement. Two afternoon sessions at an active ageing centre.

  5. Later

    Validation against a clinical reference standard

    Comparing the KneeSound score with X-ray grade (Kellgren–Lawrence) or MRI, and learning to tell OA crepitus apart from the harmless crepitus many healthy knees make.

  6. Later

    Regulatory pathway

    Intended registration with Singapore's HSA as a Class B screening aid, with the scoring algorithm assessed as Software as a Medical Device.
